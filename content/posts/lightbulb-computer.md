Ardaud argues that the future of personal computing shouldn't be glasses or headsets, which he finds constraining and socially awkward. He proposes a "Lightbulb Computer": a projector plus computer vision shaped like a bulb that screws into any standard socket, or sits in a portable lamp base. It responds to voice, sees where you point, analyzes what it sees, and projects content onto the real world.

## Use cases he demonstrates

- **Kitchen:** pin recipes and timers to surfaces, hands-free.
- **Studying:** ask questions about the book in front of you and get answers projected nearby.
- **Shared planning:** a giant projected map for trips, photos sent from a phone, board games with no setup.
- **Smart home:** point at a device to control it, a bedside ambient display, a family board in the hallway.
- **Physical objects:** scan a bookshelf with a vision model, or overlay weather and highlights on a 3D terrain map.

## Why not eyewear

He says glasses interfere with hair, jewelry and prescription lenses, are limited in power and thermals, and are antisocial because everyone needs their own compatible device. Projectors have a long research history (I/O Bulb, LuminAR, Dynamicland, Folk Computer), but none have targeted everyday personal computing.

The demo videos come from a real prototype with no editing or AI generation. He says privacy would need hardware safeguards such as an on-device camera indicator and a physical cover. Ardaud previously worked at Apple on Face ID, Apple Pencil and the iPhone camera system.
