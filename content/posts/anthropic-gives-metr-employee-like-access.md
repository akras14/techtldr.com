**Bottom line:** Anthropic has started giving a six-person team from METR, a nonprofit that evaluates frontier models, employee-like access. They get company hardware, office space and internal tools roughly on par with Anthropic's own risk assessment teams. METR will publish its analysis of Anthropic's risk levels, incidents and practices. Anthropic can't veto publication, but can require redaction of narrow predefined categories (customer data, attorney-client privileged material, model architecture). METR may say publicly whether it thinks a redaction was justified.

**Why:** Anthropic says it can't publish everything relevant to safety (some is exploitable or commercially sensitive), so it wants evaluators who understand its systems nearly as well as it does. It compares this to bank regulators placing resident examiners inside the largest banks. Evaluators need expertise, independence (no equity or financial dependence), enough access to see training and safeguards, and freedom to publish.

**Three access tiers:**
- Tier 1: narrowly scoped investigations; Anthropic picks the access and approves publication. SecureBio is here, reviewing the chemical and biological weapons chapter of the August Risk Report.
- Tier 2: evaluators publish without approval and see more, subject to redaction.
- Tier 3: METR's arrangement, employee-like access with tier 2 publication rights.

Anthropic is also working with Accenture and Faculty on a broader partnership covering red-teaming, alignment assessment and safeguard testing, and is in early talks with others including Hugging Face. It ties this to its signing of the White House Accord on Super Intelligence and says every frontier lab should embed evaluators voluntarily. METR's embedded team is firewalled from other METR staff who work with Anthropic.
