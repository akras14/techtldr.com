The post is a short response to George Hotz, who explained US lobbying to non-Americans: companies and wealthy people fund politicians' campaigns (via Super PACs, not direct bribes), the politicians pass the laws those donors want, and a helpful politician can later take a well-paid consulting job with the same donors. Hotz ends with the line Europeans often hear, that they don't get it because they aren't American.

The author says they understand the concept fine. They just don't call it lobbying. They call it corruption.

To back this up, they cite Wikipedia, which defines corruption as abuse of entrusted power for private gain and says it includes bribery, influence peddling, embezzlement and fraud, as well as practices that are legal in many countries, such as lobbying. The page even has a "legal corruption" section: power abused within the law, because those with power can write the laws that protect them.

The author's conclusion is that the two sides name the same thing differently, and that the US tends to rebrand things. In their words, the main difference is a nicer suit and a supervising lawyer.
