**Bottom line:** Fortin's claim is that passive acceptance of bad digital services isn't inevitable. Swapping a few defaults gives a noticeably better daily experience, and the network effect is a weaker excuse than people think. He says he can't escape everything, but starts with the biggest ad-driven services and moves step by step.

## His swaps

- **Search:** DuckDuckGo for more than 10 years, with no fallback to Google. For his agents he uses the Brave and Tavily APIs on free tiers.
- **Email:** ProtonMail for years; free, ad-free, with extras like a VPN and Proton Meet. Other alternatives exist.
- **Browser:** Firefox, which he says protects privacy better than Chrome, can block ads and has good developer tools. He admits it isn't perfect and lacks a sustainable business model. He calls Chrome Google's most powerful spying tool.
- **OS:** Rules of thumb: gamers use SteamOS, professionals macOS, power users and developers Linux. He is a long-time Mac user.
- **Social media:** He grants that quitting is hard because of addiction and network effects, then argues for small tweaks.

He also lists a few services he hasn't replaced yet. The piece is opinion, not a rigorous comparison.
