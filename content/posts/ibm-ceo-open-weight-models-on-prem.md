On IBM's Q3 earnings call, CEO Arvind Krishna said open-weight models are now good enough, and cheap enough, that most enterprises should run them on-prem.

**The gap.** Krishna said that in 2024 a broad benchmark might score the top closed model at 70 and an open model at 20 or 30. By late 2025 the gap had shrunk to roughly 70 versus 50. Open-weight models now cost 10 to 25 percent as much per token while being, in his words, about 90 percent as effective.

**The argument.** Most tasks (summarizing an email, reviewing a contract, answering a customer call) don't need the best model, so he expects businesses to avoid paying roughly 10x more. He thinks a useful deployment fits in two or three racks.

**The catch.** Running it is the hard part: enterprises lack kernel engineers, network specialists, and people who can place model shards on GPUs close together and tune result caching. Krishna offered no answer beyond saying IBM will ship software to ease deployment, and he expects demand to boost its Spyre mainframe accelerator cards.

**Other numbers from the call.** Infrastructure revenue rose 5% to $3.7bn, software rose 9% to $7.8bn (Red Hat up 11%), and total revenue was $17.1bn. Red Hat's virtualization products have won $800m of deals. The article also notes a severity-one IBM Classic Cloud incident that blocked new provisioning.

Note that these are an executive's claims on an earnings call, and he sells on-prem hardware and software.
