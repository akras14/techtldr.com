**Bottom line:** NetBSD, which banned AI-generated code in 2024, took in a record 120 donations totaling $120,282 last month against a $50,000 goal. The Register reads this as people who want an AI-free OS moving toward it.

- **Context:** Torvalds said in July that Linux is not an anti-AI project, and Debian developers voted in August to permit AI-generated contributions. Red Hat is moving to an "Agentic Software Development Lifecycle", and the author says AI dominated Open Source Summit Europe.
- **NetBSD's rule:** LLM-generated code is presumed "tainted" and can't be committed without prior written approval from core.
- **Other BSDs:** OpenBSD's Theo de Raadt has said AI code can't be accepted because submitters can't license it, though tmux in OpenBSD has already taken AI-assisted changes (he declined to comment). FreeBSD's core team has debated the question for over a year with no consensus. DragonFly BSD has said nothing.
- **Caveats:** The evidence is one month of donations and the author's impression of more NetBSD questions in FOSS groups. The author is openly sympathetic to the AI-averse side.
