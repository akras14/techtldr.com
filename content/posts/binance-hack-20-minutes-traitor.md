Starting around 4pm ET on October 29, attackers drained roughly $6.3 billion from Binance wallets across at least ten blockchains (including Ethereum, Bitcoin, Tron and Solana) in about 20 minutes, sending funds to addresses controlled by a single attacker. Binance suspended deposits and withdrawals, says it will cover all losses, and says it and other firms have frozen around half of the stolen funds.

**Preparation.** TRM Labs says the work began at least 36 hours earlier. The operator deployed code, tested it on two wallets, rehearsed the final version on a third and routed the test funds back, then installed the same code on a Binance hot wallet four hours later.

**Attribution.** TRM Labs and Chainalysis tie the theft to TraderTraitor, the North Korean group the FBI blamed for last year's $1.5B Bybit hack. Chainalysis saw the stolen funds mix with those from last month's Bitget exploit, already attributed to North Korea. Elliptic says North Korea is likely responsible but hasn't tied it to the group specifically. Neither Binance nor law enforcement has named a suspect.

**Entry point.** Binance first said the incident may have involved a compromised laptop, then found additional points of breach. Elliptic notes that targeting engineers is a TraderTraitor hallmark, and that the attacker funded their wallet through Tornado Cash, as in the Bybit case.
