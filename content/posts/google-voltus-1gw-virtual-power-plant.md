**Bottom line:** Google is paying other people to use less electricity so its data centers can use more, via a 1 GW "virtual power plant" with Voltus.

- The deal expands an earlier 100 MW agreement between the two companies.
- The plant is virtual: it aggregates flexible grid capacity, meaning batteries and load-shedding, into one resource that can be called on at peak demand. Load-shedding means asking industrial users to pause processes or adjusting tens of thousands of smart thermostats at once.
- Voltus pays incentives to companies that cut consumption, and to electricity resellers who can pass them on to customers.
- It covers the PJM region, the largest US grid, serving 67 million people in 13 states and Washington DC.
- Computerworld's framing is that this is a PR coup for Google: hyperscaler data centers are raising electricity prices and straining local infrastructure, which raises utilities' capital costs and bills for existing customers.
