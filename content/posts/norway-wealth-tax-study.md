Ring examines Norwegian reforms that raised dividend taxes, closed migration-related capital gains loopholes and nearly doubled the effective marginal tax rate on business wealth. The reforms drew press claims of a billionaire "exodus".

Findings:
- Migration effects are real but small: concentrated in the top 0.1% of wealth and mostly in 2022 and 2023, totaling at most 100 individuals.
- No significant outmigration or reduced inflow of startup founders or inventors.
- Revenue: without any outmigration, wealth tax revenue would have grown 75% from 2021 to 2024; with it, 71.5%.
- Firms of departing owners stay economically active and don't cut investment.
- Comparing Norwegian-owned firms (subject to the tax) with foreign-owned or tax-exempt ones, there's no evidence of higher dividend payouts, lower investment or more bankruptcies.

Ring concludes the short-run costs of wealth taxation, in migration and firm investment, are more limited than public debate suggests. The paper is a working paper and covers only the short run.
