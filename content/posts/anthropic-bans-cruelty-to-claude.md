Anthropic added "sustained and needless" cruelty toward its AI to the list of forbidden conduct in its usage policy, next to bullying others, promoting self-harm and creating non-consensual intimate imagery. The company says its tools could already end interactions in "rare" cases, and the policy only targets extreme, repeated abuse.

- Anthropic says the rule does not cover ordinary frustration, pushback, dark creative themes, or model testing and research. What counts as "abusive or cruel" is still unclear.
- The change came out of an annual policy review, which also banned "deceptive campaigns" and clarified that Claude can't be used to deceive voters or disrupt elections.
- Reaction was mixed. Some praised it as good manners or "model welfare." Critics called it "deeply wrong," or argued it dilutes concern about abuse of humans and animals. A technology lawyer said anthropomorphizing AI is harmful because it leads people to think it is something it isn't. Microsoft AI's Mustafa Suleyman answered with a melting-face emoji.
- The article ties this to the older debate over whether being polite to chatbots helps. Some experts say "please" wastes tokens; others say politeness gets better answers, and that rudeness to bots could spill into how people treat each other.
