A proposed Department of Homeland Security rule would make universities pay about $100,000 per graduate who uses Optional Practical Training (OPT) to work in the US after their degree. DHS projects OPT enrollment could fall by up to 44%, while an economist's analysis suggests master's-level participation would be virtually eliminated.

## Key points

- OPT lets international students work in the US for up to 3 years after a degree. About 420,000 students, roughly a third of international students, used it in 2025–26, making it the biggest pipeline of global talent into the US workforce.
- The charge would be $70,000 for the first year, plus $30,000 more for STEM graduates' two-year extension. DHS says the rule is meant to combat fraud and protect US workers, and it estimates universities would pay $8.4–16.5 billion a year.
- Michael Clemens of Johns Hopkins projects bachelor's-level enrollment would halve and master's-level enrollment nearly vanish. Colleges could lose $2.5–4.2 billion a year in tuition and other revenue. About 70% of international STEM PhD graduates used OPT in 2022.
- Combined with the 2025 $100,000 levy on a researcher visa, which cut new applications by 90%, Clemens says the measures "will obliterate the pipeline." A National Academies analysis found the US STEM workforce could shrink 6–11% over a decade if all proposals took effect.
- Scholars told Nature the policy would change who chooses to come, since top prospects can go elsewhere.
