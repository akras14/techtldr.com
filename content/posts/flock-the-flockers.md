Anthony Sistilli, a software engineer and YouTuber in Brampton, Ontario, built his own license plate recognition camera that tracked only police cars, and two Peel Regional Police officers then showed up at his door to ask about his "intentions" for the data. By his account, the officers said that data on where they live and when they work could put them at risk, which is the same concern critics raise about police ALPR systems like Flock.

## What happened

- Brampton recently spent C$2 million on high-resolution cameras with automated license plate recognition.
- About a month ago, Sistilli built a personal ALPR camera, described as a way to "flock the flockers," and posted TikTok videos on how it works.
- Earlier this week he posted a YouTube video saying two Peel Regional Police officers visited him. He says one of them noted how scary it would be for police if people could learn where they live, when their shifts start and what they do during the day.
- Peel Regional Police had not responded to Gizmodo's request for comment.

## The wider discussion

Gizmodo notes the irony: ALPR data has been misused to stalk ex-partners, harass immigrants and wrongly target random people, so police know the risks of such data better than most. Sistilli's long YouTube video uses the visit to look at how Flock markets to municipalities, whether the cameras reduce crime, and the lack of transparency around data collection and retention.
