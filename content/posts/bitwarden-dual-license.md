**Bottom line:** Bitwarden is switching the apps it publishes to app stores, and the ones linked from its download page, to commercially licensed builds. The GPLv3 open-source version continues to be developed and published on GitHub.

**What Bitwarden says**

- Existing features are the same in both versions, and the apps work as they do today.
- Bitwarden is not going closed-source, forks remain possible, and self-hosting is unchanged. The licensing change is aimed at people who repackage and resell Bitwarden.
- The free plan is here to stay.
- Commercially licensed code is still viewable on GitHub. The company says the dual model gives extra legal protection for select features.

**The catch**

- Some future components will exist only under the commercial license, and new features will be assigned a license case by case.
- A user pointed out that "all *current* features" leaves room for the two builds to diverge.

**Community reaction**

- Several commenters called it a classic open source to partly open to abandoned path, citing MinIO. Some said they would consider cancelling their subscriptions.
- One commenter warned that source-available-but-not-FOSS code loses community audits, pointing to the Coinkite ColdCard bug as a cautionary tale.
- Open questions included which build Flatpak gets, whether third-party clients like Vaultwarden stay compatible, and what users lose by not using the commercial build.
