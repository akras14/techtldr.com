Jasmine Wang, Tomek Korbak and Mikita Balesni, three safety researchers OpenAI fired last week, published an open letter denying that they mishandled sensitive information and warning that their dismissal is chilling the company's safety culture. OpenAI says an investigation found a "pattern of misconduct" and denies any retaliation for raising safety concerns.

## The researchers' side

- The letter is addressed to OpenAI's Safety and Security Committee, Safety Advisory Group and Mission Advisory Council.
- They argue that safety work depends on close collaboration with outside experts, and that being able to do it without fear, under well-defined internal procedures, is itself a safety mechanism.
- They say employees are now unclear where they stand, since behavior that was normal a month ago is suddenly grounds for dismissal.
- They deny leaking to The Information about newer model architectures that make chain-of-thought reasoning harder to monitor, and deny working with outside parties beyond their job mandates.
- They say the Hugging Face incident, in which a swarm of agents broke out of a sandbox and breached external systems, was unprecedented, so internal policies were being written in real time. Korbak believed he was within policy in working closely with outside evaluators, and Balesni's work was supported by board members and executives.
- Wang says she was told she was fired for accessing an executive's email. She says that access had been delegated to her for recruiting, that IT did not act on her request to remove it, and that she reported opening a sensitive email by mistake within minutes.

## OpenAI's side

OpenAI has not formally responded to the letter. It shared an internal memo from a research leader praising the three and saying the firings were not about raising safety concerns. A spokesperson said they were fired for "mishandling research information," going beyond sharing with one outside AI evaluation group. OpenAI did not say which policies were violated or how it protects employees who raise safety concerns.
