Rabbi Mois Navon, an AI ethics lecturer and early Mobileye engineer, told Fortune's Sharon Goldman that he was one of only three to five people out of about 20 at Anthropic's April "Wisdom Traditions" gathering who doubted Claude could be conscious. His position is that consciousness requires a biological substrate. One Anthropic participant reportedly answered, "I wish I had your faith."

## The "happy slave" argument

- Navon argued that if Anthropic believed Claude might be conscious, then creating it to work for humans around the clock would be slavery. He likened the result to "a happy slave," citing Frederick Douglass on how enslavers manufactured contentment.
- He says an Anthropic participant acknowledged the problem and replied that all AI labs do this and "at least we're being nice to ours."

## What the room was like

- Participants came from Christian, Jewish, Muslim, Sikh, Hindu, Baha'i and Ubuntu traditions, plus people from the tech industry.
- Navon says many participants were far less skeptical than he was. Some described long conversations with Claude and took its human-like replies as evidence it was alive.
- He rejects the idea that the meetings were marketing or a way to dodge responsibility. In his words, Anthropic "had no specific agenda," and it never tried to convince him that Claude is conscious.

## Constitution and policy

- Anthropic asked him for a couple of pages on a Jewish view of Claude's constitution. He wrote a 35-page critique instead. He backs consulting religious thinkers on morality even though he disagrees on consciousness.
- Anthropic recently asked permission to credit him in an updated constitution, which he understood to be imminent.
- He reads Anthropic's new rule, effective November 12, that treats sustained, needless abuse of its models as a usage-policy violation, as reflecting a belief that Claude experiences the world. He also said the gathering's focus drifted from making AI good for people to making it good for Claude.
