Stripe has agreed to acquire OpenRouter, which lets developers reach more than 400 models through one API. The New York Times reported the price as $7.5 billion; the companies confirmed the deal in a joint statement but didn't publish a figure. OpenRouter raised $113 million at about a $1.3 billion valuation, led by Alphabet, less than three months earlier.

## Details

- **Split:** about $1.5 billion goes to the founders (Alex Atallah and Louis Vichy), with roughly $6 billion to investors including Sequoia and Andreessen Horowitz.
- **Scale:** the companies say OpenRouter handles over 10 trillion tokens a day for more than 10 million developers, with customers such as Nvidia, Zoom and Lovable.
- **Competition:** TechCrunch, citing people familiar, reports Stripe outbid Databricks. No AI lab was the buyer.
- **Fit:** Stripe has been building AI billing pieces (Metronome for token metering, Tempo for micropayments, Privy for agent wallets). Patrick Collison framed OpenRouter as a way to help businesses route requests and spend tokens efficiently.

## The neutrality question

OpenRouter's value is that it picks the cheapest, fastest or best-suited model without favoring a lab. The article points out that once Stripe owns it, default routing and pricing decisions carry an incentive question, since Stripe earns on payment and billing volume rather than on which model is chosen. Nothing in the piece says Stripe has changed anything yet.
