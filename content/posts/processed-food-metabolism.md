**Bottom line:** In a small Virginia Tech study, two meals matched within 1% on calories, carbs, fat, protein, water and salt produced different metabolic and brain responses depending on how processed they were. The ultraprocessed meal drove a higher insulin response, more energy expenditure, less carbohydrate burned for fuel, and different activity in the brain's reward system. It suggests processing itself, not just nutrient content, may matter, but the sample is small and the meals differ in other ways.

## What they did

- 57 healthy adults aged 18 to 45 took part; 32 completed both the metabolic and fMRI sessions.
- Each person ate both a minimally and a highly processed 300-calorie meal, in separate sessions at least three days apart, classified using the Nova scale.
- The ultraprocessed meal: peanut butter and jelly sandwich, deli turkey, veggie chips, a cookie, cereal, instant mashed potatoes. The minimally processed meal: banana, dried cranberries, cheese, egg.
- Metabolism was measured in a sealed metabolic chamber after an overnight fast, with blood draws over three hours. On another day, fMRI scans tracked brain responses to pictures of the foods, and participants said what they would pay for each.

## Findings

- Every metabolic measure differed. After the ultraprocessed meal, insulin was higher, blood sugar stayed up a bit longer, energy expenditure was higher, and less carbohydrate was burned.
- Differences in carbohydrate burning were linked to differences in how the ventral striatum and nucleus accumbens (motivation and reward) responded to food pictures.
- Stated willingness to pay did not differ between the two kinds of food.
- Published in Nature Metabolism. An outside expert, Carlos Monteiro (creator of Nova), called the brain-activity angle novel and important.

## Caveats

- One pair of 300-calorie meals, in young, healthy people.
- Protein sources differed, the ultraprocessed meal had more additives, and the food's physical and chemical structure wasn't measured, so any of these could contribute.
- Next steps: other populations, longer periods, bigger meals, more food pairings, and isolating specific additives or processing steps.
