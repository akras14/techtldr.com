Lozano-Robledo wrote this after a desperate message from a student asking whether math academia will shrink to only the brightest minds. His worry is that a generation of mathematicians could be lost as students drop PhD plans over AI. His TL;DR: keep calm and carry on studying math.

He frames it with two disclaimers: his answers may age badly, and no one knows what comes next. He says to distrust anyone who is very confident, and notes that frontier AI companies shape the narrative before their IPOs. They announce solved problems while hiding the cost of failed attempts.

His answers to the student questions:

- **Will AI surpass human mathematicians?** Unclear. Proofs so far contain no alien, move-37 ideas. He likes a toy model in which LLMs stay within the convex hull of existing ideas, so humans must add new ideas to expand it. Cost and ethics may also cap what is worth solving by brute force.
- **Will mathematicians become prompters?** No sign of it. Working with an LLM resembles collaborating, and results depend on the mathematician's guidance. Mathematicians care about why a result is true.
- **Only the brightest?** Mathematics has always had room for varied abilities, and the field has many under-explored areas.
- **Employable and needed?** Departments exist to discover and communicate mathematics, and the education mission continues.

Personally, he says his days look much as before, except he is busier, with triple the projects and more student collaborators, since LLMs help students learn faster and join research earlier.
