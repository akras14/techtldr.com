Peter Bryant's bottom line: software engineers have been skilled tradespeople all along, and thinking of themselves as white-collar is part of why AI feels like an identity crisis.

- **The framing:** Developers do what builders, plumbers and electricians do (make or fix things), just in bits rather than atoms. Years of practice matter, formal education doesn't strictly.
- **Why it matters:** Blue-collar trades have ridden past waves of technology by learning to operate the new machines. White-collar workers usually bear the most disruption, and developers are discovering they can be outpaced by technology too.
- **The end of the honeymoon:** After 30 years as rock stars (protected attention, outsized salaries, unquestioned estimates), developers are no longer the most important people in the company. He compares them to seamstresses facing the loom or accountants facing Excel.
- **What to do:** Learn the new tools, as other trades did. Hand craft remains available as something to enjoy, even if it is no longer something you can be paid for.
