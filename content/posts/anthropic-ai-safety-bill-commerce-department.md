**Bottom line:** According to Politico, as relayed by Tom's Hardware, Anthropic is quietly lobbying for a federal bill that would let the government block an AI model's release only when it poses a risk of $1 billion in damages or of killing or injuring more than 50 people.

- **Evaluation:** an independent third party registered with the Department of Commerce would periodically evaluate the lab and its models. The lab may pay the evaluators as long as their independence isn't affected.
- **Covered risks:** bioweapons, AI-enabled cyberattacks, and a lab losing control of a model or agents.
- **Process:** if the evaluator sees a chance of such an event, it must report to Commerce within 72 hours. Commerce then brings the evidence to a federal judge in the D.C. District Court, who decides whether to halt the release.
- **Exclusivity:** no other department, agency or officer could order a lab to stop releasing a model.

Anthropic says the bill reflects its Advanced AI Framework, which it has advocated to federal and state lawmakers since June, and calls these "sensible policies" for Congress. The article places it alongside other pending proposals, including a bipartisan kill-switch bill and a Sanders proposal for a 20-year prison term for developing superintelligence. The piece is a short news summary with a worried tone; the bill text itself isn't linked.
