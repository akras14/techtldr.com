**Bottom line:** Water is becoming the flashpoint for data center siting. Paris Marx argues that hyperscale facilities, and especially GPU-heavy AI ones, compete with residents for drinking water, and that communities are starting to win concessions by organizing.

- **Scale:** A hyperscale data center can use 1 to 5 million gallons a day. GPUs run hotter than traditional servers, so cooling needs rise. Facilities also need clean water, and tapping the public supply is cheaper than treating their own.
- **Chile:** Google's planned $200M data center in Cerrillos (Santiago) projected 169 liters per second, about a day's worth equal to what all 80,000 residents drink in a year. It would have drawn from an aquifer where new extraction had just been banned. A resident referendum in December 2019 came out 49% against and 38% in favor. In 2022 Google came back with an air-cooled design. Microsoft's Quilicura project drew similar opposition.
- **Uruguay:** During the worst drought in 70 years, a researcher had to go to court to learn that Google's Canelones site planned about 2 million gallons a day.
- **Brazil:** Several large data centers are planned near Fortaleza, including a roughly $10B one reportedly serving TikTok, in a region with emergency drought conditions in 16 of 21 years.

Marx says the pattern is global and is now showing up in the US and Europe too. The piece is an opinionated excerpt rather than a survey, and the quoted figures come from company filings and local researchers.
