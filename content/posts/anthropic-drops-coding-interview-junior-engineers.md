Felix Rieseberg, a hiring manager at Anthropic, says he has dropped the traditional coding interview. Small coding challenges used to be a weak but useful predictor of success, but there is now no more point in a little React challenge than in quizzing people on assembly, because Claude is better at both. He reports that relatively new engineers using AI are overtaking more experienced, arguably better coders: "hunger beats tenure."

## Why newcomers have an edge

- Fresh eyes spot friction that veterans stopped noticing. At Notion, Rieseberg was told offline mode was impossible; the data-model complexity was real, but the founder's supposed resistance was never actually checked.
- The catch is that juniors lack status, and companies are built so that a few people green-light ideas. That used to force a long grind before anyone would bet on your idea.
- With AI, execution is no longer expensive, so a working prototype can replace the usual PRD and buy-in process.

## His advice

1. Pick business impact over your backlog: attach yourself to the parts of the company that make money.
2. Pick a radical idea that would substantially help the organization reach its goals.
3. Build a working prototype over a weekend.
4. Focus on one good idea; don't bury it among twenty bad ones in the same prototype.
5. Lean into your fresh perspective as a user, or as someone who never learned why things are "impossible."
