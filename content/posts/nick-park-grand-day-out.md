Park started A Grand Day Out in 1982 at the National Film and Television School and, by his own account, did "probably 90% of it myself". He assumed a ten-minute film would take six months. One script paragraph about building a rocket took a year and a half.

Details from the piece:
- Gromit began as a cat and Wallace had a mustache. Gromit became a dog because dogs were easier to model, and his dialogue was cut in the first week of shooting, which led Park to express everything through the eyes.
- Wallace's wide "coathanger mouth" came from animating the actor's pronunciation of "cheese".
- Park drew on Eastern European animators (Starewicz, Trnka, Švankmajer) and live-action filmmaking, using lighting and slow camera drift to give the model animation a feature-film feel.
- By 1985 only seven minutes were done and his grant had run out. Aardman put him on salary and let him keep working on the film in spare hours behind a curtain.

After premiering at the Bristol festival in 1989 alongside Creature Comforts, the film won a BAFTA, and Park quickly became a star. The "Nick Park look" went on to dominate Aardman. The newsletter issue also covers an education project and animation news.
