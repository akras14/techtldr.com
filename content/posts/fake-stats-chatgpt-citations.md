A GEO researcher showed that fabricated statistics can persist in ChatGPT's retrieval and spread into real citations. In March 2026 he generated 50,000 statistics pages with GPT-4.1 on a new domain, stateglobe.com, none of them checked, then added 15,000 listicles. Total cost was about $50.

**Google vs. ChatGPT.** Google indexed the site briefly, then its visibility collapsed after two updates. ChatGPT never stopped using it. Cloudflare logs show 600,000+ bot requests in the last 30 days, about 72,000 of them from ChatGPT-User, the agent that fetches pages live to answer a question. GA4 shows 10,000+ sessions from ChatGPT, and on Oct 7 the site ranked second in visibility for the author's tracked prompts, behind Statista.

**Citation laundering.** Ahrefs and Semrush show 330+ referring domains, including Shopify, DHL, and Bluehost. The invented numbers were also cited in an academic paper, a Qatari financial group's investment research, an Indian economics publication, and a Canadian community report. Each citation makes the figure look more credible, and the author warns they could eventually reach training data.

**Takeaways.** Being retrievable by an LLM is a different problem from ranking on Google. The author, who works for an AI-search analytics vendor, frames this as research rather than a playbook and says writers should verify sources. A later test with fully verified stats also attracted links, mostly from sites he suspects are AI-written.

The results are self-reported from the author's own analytics.
