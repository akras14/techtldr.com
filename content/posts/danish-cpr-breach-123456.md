**Bottom line:** Hackers reached Denmark's central civil registration (CPR) database through a tiny Funen-based IT firm, Pays ApS, whose accounts, including the administrator account, reportedly used the password "123456". The breach exposed data tied to about 8.8 million CPR numbers, and the attacker had access for roughly 21 days.

## What happened

- Politiken reviewed data allegedly used by the hacker and found at least three Pays user accounts with the password "123456", one of them the administrator account.
- Pays, an Odense company with two employees as of July 2026, confirmed to TV 2 that its legitimate access to search the CPR system was abused.
- Access ran from 10 September for a total of 21 days and 17 hours.
- The CPR holds personal information on people living or formerly registered in Denmark. Private companies and associations can get access if they have a legitimate need, such as looking up customer addresses.

## How the hacker says they got in

An anonymous hacker told Politiken they were behind the attack and that it was not particularly difficult. They say they started with a leaked password from a former employee of a small Danish company, then wrote two programs to pull data from the CPR system and store it externally. They claim there are no plans to sell or publish the data. These claims are unverified.

## Reaction

Aarhus University professor Jens Myrup Pedersen called the firm's password security "hopeless" and said it was "an open door", adding that it was only a matter of time before things went wrong.
