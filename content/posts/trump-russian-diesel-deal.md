Trump says Putin has agreed to release 300,000 tonnes of diesel to the US and global market immediately, followed by 500,000 tonnes in November and a million more after that. Zelensky condemned the deal as a gift to Putin, "an investment in a war that must be ended, not prolonged". Analysts say the volumes are too small to move US pump prices.

## Details

- The US Treasury is understood to be suspending sanctions on Russian diesel exports until 7 April and has issued a temporary licence. Other Russian assets in American banks remain frozen.
- Trump said a further three million tonnes would follow "within a short period", depending on the condition of Russian refineries, which Ukraine has attacked.
- Putin said Russia is willing to supply oil products to the US and global markets, but gave no amounts. The BBC has asked the White House what Russia gets in return.
- Russia banned diesel exports earlier this year after two waves of fuel shortages caused by Ukrainian drone strikes. The IEA estimates its diesel output has fallen nearly 30%, so analysts question whether it can deliver these quantities.

## Reactions

- Zelensky said Russia would repay the diesel with "further terror" and defended the refinery strikes as a response to attacks on Ukraine's energy sector.
- The UK government said its position "could not be clearer" and that it will keep up sanctions pressure on Russia.
- Quilter Cheviot's Tim Armitage said the first tranche is about 2.25 million barrels against US daily consumption of about 3.8 million, so it "won't make a material difference". He called it a "slightly desperate move" to lower inflation before the midterms.

## Context

US diesel averages $6.28 a gallon, down from a record $6.53 at the end of September, after prices rose sharply following the Iran war that began in February. Brent crude is above $103 a barrel, against about $73 before the war. The deal reverses recent policy, since Congress just passed legislation authorizing new sanctions and tariffs on countries that import Russian oil and gas. The BBC notes it also invites criticism that the US is helping fund Moscow's war.
