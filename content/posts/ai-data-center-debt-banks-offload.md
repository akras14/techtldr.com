**Bottom line:** The banks that financed the AI data center build-out are starting to hedge. Per the article, they are nearing internal exposure limits on AI-linked loans and exploring significant risk transfers, which move the riskiest slice of a loan to outside investors. This is a secondhand roundup of FT and Bloomberg reporting, with some editorializing.

## Reported details

- **$38B stuck:** a bank group including JPMorgan and MUFG has spent 6+ months trying to distribute construction debt for a Texas and Wisconsin data center project leased to Oracle. Some lenders will sell pieces to non-banks at a discount.
- **Oracle:** five-year CDS around 151 bp in early October, levels last seen in 2009 (a caption elsewhere on the page cites 203 bp). S&P cut it to BBB-, one notch above junk, in July. It reportedly targets over $40B more debt and equity this fiscal year.
- **Weaker demand:** order books for Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Microsoft and Oracle bonds fell from about 5x to under 2x covered between February and July.
- **Rates:** the 10-year Treasury yield hit 5.304%, highest since 2002. The Fed's September minutes cited AI-related borrowing as a contributor, though Capital Economics blames inflation and deficits more.
- **Credit tiers:** CoreWeave-leased paper prices at 7.00% to 9.875% versus 5.70% to 7.875% for hyperscaler-leased bonds, so smaller AI infrastructure firms face costlier money.

The author's read is that the AI boom assumes credit stays open at reasonable terms, and that assumption is now being tested.
