Over the past couple of weeks, hundreds of AI-decompiled emulators and game ports have appeared, and Parker argues the cause is Claude Opus 5.5, which he says is extremely good at decompiling. Reconstructing a game's source, which used to take coders months, can now be left running overnight.

The latest batch is browser ports of Call of Duty: Black Ops, Halo: CE, GTA: Vice City, Skate 3 and The Simpsons: Hit and Run, shared in a viral post. Parker played several and found some close to flawless. Hit and Run can even target 240fps. One of four Halo: CE ports he tried had working online servers with low ping.

Most of the games are PS2- or PS3-era titles, and Parker notes the PS3 had about 512 MB of RAM while his browser was using about 1,200 MB, so memory is no longer the limit for browser ports. What changed is the cost of the decompiling work.

Publishers can send cease-and-desist letters, but Parker points out the ports are quick to create, so takedowns look like whack-a-mole at a scale he compares to pirated DVDs. He says he doesn't know how the genie goes back in the bottle, and expects publishers' legal teams to be busy.
