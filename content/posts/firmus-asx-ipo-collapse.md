**Bottom line:** Firmus Technologies, an AI datacentre company, withdrew its planned ASX listing on Friday after starting the week with an IPO pitched at $11 a share, $7bn raised and a $44bn valuation. The demand bankers claimed on Monday never showed up, and the Guardian, citing anonymous sources, says retail investors were probably spared a bad outcome.

**How the week went:**

- **Monday:** Bankers (Morgans, Morgan Stanley, JP Morgan, Bank of America) told investors demand was "well in excess of the offer size". The valuation was roughly Woolworths-sized for a startup with two small operating sites that was valued under $2bn in 2025. About half the deal was going to existing strategic investors.
- **Tuesday:** Investment houses grew uneasy because well over 50% of the register, including holdings of Blackstone, Jane Street and Coatue, could be sold right after the 23 October listing. Even founder Oliver Curtis, thought to be locked in by escrow, could reportedly monetise most of his stake through a financial instrument. That raised the worry that retail investors would be the early investors' exit.
- **Wednesday:** Demand from Australian and US investors fell short, so bankers sought US$150m bids from overseas hedge funds, which is a sign of desperation. They tried repricing to $9 and cutting the raise from about $7bn to just over $4bn. Super funds weren't interested.
- **Thursday:** The price fell to $5.50, half the original. Each change meant re-confirming commitments. Firmus also pulled out of a parliamentary AI inquiry appearance.
- **Friday:** Firmus withdrew, blaming "market volatility", even though tech indices were near all-time highs. It says it will seek private capital or other options.

**Why it matters.** Almost all of Firmus's expected revenue depends on datacentres that aren't built yet (seven contracted, four planned), so it needs funding. One investment manager said that without access to funding "they don't have a business", and private investors who expected the IPO as their exit may be stuck. Recent datacentre float DigiCo has lost more than half its value since late 2024. A fund manager quoted in the piece said the listing "was going to end in tears".
