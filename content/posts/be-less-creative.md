Wolf never finished a song as a teenager because he refused to use choices other people had made, fearing it would make the song less his own. At a jam session with a musician friend, Jon, he saw a different method.

They picked a feeling to write about (flow, which became the chorus hook), built a playlist of songs with the right energy, and studied them. Shared tempo and chord progressions were adopted. They went further, turning a guitar riff into a vocal rhythm and splicing a missing hi-hat onto a borrowed-sounding drum loop. Everything was a sketch, so they tried five or six melodies and kept what worked. Jon's rule: make decisions in service of the song, not the parts.

Wolf traces his old blockage to scarcity thinking, the fear of having only a few ideas. Jon showed abundance: a great song comes through hundreds of mediocre ones, and pop songs share chords yet sound different.

He ties this to engineering: building custom tools instead of choosing boring technology, and startups that need several miracles to succeed. His takeaway is that your creative expression can be narrow and the whole work can still be yours.
