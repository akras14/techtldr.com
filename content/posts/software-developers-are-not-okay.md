Bjarnason's claim is that the software industry has become an increasingly hostile place to work, and that this is driven by managerial and executive incentives, not just by AI. He thinks this is about as good as it gets for developers at big tech companies and advises considering a future outside tech.

## The argument

- He points to a wave of "I'm leaving tech" videos with huge comment sections, plus students switching away from computer science and UX. Iceland's CS sign-ups reportedly fell by about a third year over year. Many of the people leaving embraced AI first and then found the work no longer enjoyable.
- Executives gain from fewer staff: fewer stock options to pay, less internal resistance to unethical projects, and a weaker labour force. Meta's attempt to replace developers with AI was dialled back, but the intent to cut headcount remains.
- Workloads and stress rise as teams shrink. Agent-driven work feels like delegating to a clueless junior rather than using a power tool, which weakens ownership and flow. Much new software is also AI features or clones, so there is less novelty.
- Constant layoff risk and "skill issue" blame keep workers fearful and unable to negotiate. People who quit preempt severance and are replaced from a large pool of unemployed developers.
- Models only need to "sort of work" for this to continue, so a bubble collapse or higher model costs would not fix it. Costs could be shifted to developers, for example through hardware requirements.

He also expects many people to face identity crises if their sense of self is tied to being a programmer.
