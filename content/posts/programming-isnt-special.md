Writers, musicians and visual artists have largely organized against generative AI, yet many experienced programmers accept it, often on the grounds that code is functional rather than art. Glyph says that premise is wrong: programming is art, and the same case against AI applies.

- **Art is often mundane.** Drawing on John Berger's idea of "mystification", Glyph notes that we venerate novels and paintings while looking down on journalism and copywriting, though the skills overlap and the split is mostly commercial accident. Most writing, visual art and music is workaday, and most code is too.
- **Code can be beautiful.** Glyph describes Twisted's `Deferred` as an aesthetic reaction to the tedium of passing callbacks around, and traces its influence through jQuery Deferred and Promises to async/await. Deliberately designed software tends to matter socially and technologically.
- **Defend the mundane.** Mundane projects are where people get the practice to eventually do great work. If AI takes over all of them, the already small chance (Glyph guesses 0.1% per project) of a career-defining piece drops from "sometimes" to "never".

Glyph is not against abstraction or automation, which are core to programming. His objection is to using AI to remove the understanding and decision-making rather than raise them a level. He leaves open how much to resist, and says only that it is worth resisting in software as much as in any creative medium.
