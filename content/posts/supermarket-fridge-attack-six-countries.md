**Bottom line:** Around 40 Delhaize stores in Belgium and Luxembourg, plus smaller numbers of shops in Sweden, Ireland, the Netherlands and Estonia, had refrigerators and freezers attacked overnight. No one has claimed responsibility.

**Method.** Sabcobel, the Belgian maker of some of the affected units, says the evaporators were switched into defrost mode, which warms the units. It believes this is the same method as the early-October attack on hundreds of French supermarkets (see our [earlier post](/european-supermarket-fridge-cyberattack/)). Temperatures reportedly reached 50°C.

**Fix.** Sabcobel's CEO says the company has worked since the French attack to protect customer fridges, but the units need physical modifications, not only software updates. Its simplest advice is to manage fridges over a secure internet connection, which most customers adopted after France. Affected units were disconnected from the network.

**Impact.**
- Belgium: Delhaize says 15 of its Belgian stores were affected, and one branch reported about €100,000 in lost produce. Colruyt says five Spar shops were hit.
- Sweden: ICA stores operating outside its central network and 7 of Coop's 800 stores were affected.
- Ireland: 13 SuperValu stores, limited to a specific refrigerator model.
- Netherlands: Danfoss told NOS that at least 17 companies, mostly butchers and neighborhood shops, were hit.
- Estonia: a Selver supermarket and reportedly others.

Belgian and Swedish national cyber agencies are monitoring.
