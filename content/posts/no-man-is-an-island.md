Borretti started from the plan that he would manage AI agents at work and keep doing the intellectual side of software (reading, learning and designing languages, writing essays, tinkering on open source) for fun. The second half did not survive.

His software example has two parts:

- **Worse discourse.** Talk shifted from compilers and type systems to prompts, harnesses and loops. He says prompting is a much shallower skill than programming, so little human capital is being built.
- **A pointless commons.** A blog post, library or new language now mostly feeds the next training run, and the humans making decisions no longer read the code. You can grow a private garden with AI, but there is no shared human project to contribute to.

He generalizes this. Intellectual activity needs a shared body of work to build on and motivation. Intrinsic motivation is real but fleeting, so extrinsic motivation (usefulness to others, esteem of peers) fills the gaps. Private work sustains the community, and the community sustains private work.

After AI, he says, contributions become redundant. Mathematics follows the same path if AI proves theorems, writes papers and textbooks, and tutors students. The result would be individuals working alone, without a living community of programmers or mathematicians.

He rejects the view that this just filters out clout-chasers and leaves the intrinsically motivated. Humans are social animals who want their work to matter to others, and if technology makes their contributions superfluous, he asks what is left.
