The Proofs and Prompts hosts collected a snapshot of the math community's gut reactions to OpenAI's 6 October release of papers claiming solutions to 100+ open problems. Each note is signed by name only. The page stays open for new contributions for another week. The tone ranges from awe to bewilderment to anger.

- **Awe at the results.** Contributors in group theory and topology describe results that look like almost all the major open problems in their fields, including claims about Thompson's group F, the Boone–Higman conjecture, and the Borel conjecture in dimension 4. One says a result he thought was science fiction has now arrived, maybe a year sooner than he expected.
- **Verification and quality.** Several say the write-ups vary wildly in quality, some are unverified, and checking them will take months. OpenAI provided only partial Lean certificates.
- **Norms.** Critics object that the papers name no authors, aren't submitted to journals, and come with no information about how problems were selected or how many attempts failed. One writer says an OpenAI-controlled GitHub repo with issues and pull requests disabled can't serve as a place to discuss the results.
- **A more charitable view.** One mathematician argues the papers are a byproduct of OpenAI evaluating internal models on hard open problems, and credits the company for consulting an Advisory Group on Mathematics and AI. OpenAI followed some of its advice, such as a timely release, but plans to keep using open problems for model development.
- **Worries about the field.** Many fear for junior mathematicians and PhD students: if solving problems was the craft, what is the incentive to learn it? Others note that attempts on open problems often yield new discoveries, which may now be lost. Several argue mathematicians must widen their idea of the job toward understanding and choosing good conjectures.
