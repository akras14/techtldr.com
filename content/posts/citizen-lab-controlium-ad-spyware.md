Citizen Lab says an Israeli ad agency, Discover-Tech, runs a platform called Controlium that profiles millions of users through ordinary ads and then lets spyware vendors silently infect chosen targets, with no click needed. Researchers call it the first concrete technical evidence of ad-tech being used for automated, targeted zero-click hacking.

## How it works

- Controlium serves seemingly benign custom ads (for example, a game or a telecom service) and uses them to harvest a viewer's identity and IP address, which Citizen Lab says lets clients "mass-profile and deanonymise" users.
- If a viewer is picked as a target, a third-party "mercenary" spyware firm can push malicious code to the device. Infection takes seconds and needs no interaction, which Citizen Lab calls a new delivery technique.
- The spyware can reportedly read messages, photos and email, record calls and switch on microphones.

## Scale and victims

- Discover-Tech has served ads to millions of users since 2024, including in Yemen, Brazil, Mexico and Czechia.
- Citizen Lab believes dozens of devices at Al Jazeera were compromised this way.
- Separate work by Confiant suggests the ads reached networks of European and US government bodies, the UN, NATO, the ICC and the IAEA.
- Discover-Tech had been approached for comment and had not replied at publication.

## Why it matters

Programmatic ad auctions run in a split second among dozens or hundreds of bidders using profile data, with little regulation. Researcher Wolfie Christl says major ad vendors failed to detect or stop this for years, and calls for regulators and cybersecurity agencies to investigate the companies involved and fix the systemic risk.
