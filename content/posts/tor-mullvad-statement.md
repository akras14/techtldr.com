Community members asked how working with Mullvad fits Tor's human rights mission after a Mullvad co-founder's political donation. Tor says it reached its decision after due diligence: discussions with community, staff and board, a staff survey, and financial analysis and scenario planning. The post does not describe the donation itself.

What Tor will do:

- **Continue the technical collaboration.** It cites a better structured, more maintainable and more auditable Tor Browser codebase, feedback from users beyond Tor's core audience, a lower-risk place to test features, and wider access to a free open-source privacy tool. It says human-rights privacy work is badly under-resourced and sometimes requires hard compromises.
- **Pause proactive co-branding** with Mullvad. The narrowly scoped agreement continues.
- **Revise its language** about Mullvad Browser on its own channels so it does not suggest shared values or implied endorsement.

Tor says it strongly opposes rhetoric that threatens other human rights, and that not all speech fits its mission. It admits the decision will not satisfy everyone and costs some trust, and says the concerns raised will shape its path.
