Nate Meyvis offers conjectural answers to the common skeptic's question of why AI coding hasn't produced a wave of new, good software. He flags that some answers deserve little weight.

- **It is showing up, just not as new products:** fewer bugs, better performance, and more features in existing products. Most people adopt new software rarely, so the "we" seeing it is limited.
- **Slow organizations:** many companies have political subsystems, such as sign-off from senior engineers and PMs, that AI hasn't sped up.
- **Software is more ephemeral or private:** doing your taxes with Claude replaces shopping for better tax software, and domain experts, executives and companies build bespoke tools nobody else sees.
- **Quantity over quality:** teams may favor making more software over improving what exists.
- **Speedup taken as leisure:** he jokes that teams offer their companies 2x while using tools that allow 10x, and take long coffee breaks.
- **Less software, sometimes:** AI can show a project is a bad idea or push people toward analog time. Often what improves is a system with software and non-software parts.
