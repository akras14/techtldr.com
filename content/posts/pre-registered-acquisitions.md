**Bottom line:** Alex Wang argues that AI "neolabs" are asked to deliver both a research breakthrough and a venture-scale business, which compounds two long shots. He proposes borrowing from pharma: frontier labs would announce acquisition prices for specific results in advance ("pre-registered acquisitions"), so neolabs can focus on research and investors have a known buyer.

## The problem

- Frontier labs (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind) are in a tight race and prefer to scale what already works, even though their researchers want to chase new paradigms.
- Neolabs, startups founded by senior researchers and funded heavily before having a product, are expected to make a breakthrough and build a business. The author puts each at roughly 1 in 100, so both together at 1 in 10,000.
- Simply letting them do pure research and get acquired doesn't fit VC math, since funds need outcomes that return the whole fund at multibillion valuations.

## The pharma model

Small biotechs take the risk of discovery and development, and big pharma buys what works and handles manufacturing and distribution. Of FDA approvals from 2013 to 2022, companies with under $500M revenue originated 52%, versus 36% for those over $10B.

## The proposal

Pre-registered acquisitions are a form of "pull funding": paying for a result once it exists, as with the 1714 longitude prize or the 2009 pneumococcal vaccine commitment. Two forms:

- **Open offers:** a lab publishes a verifiable target and commits to acquire the first team to hit it at a set price. Example: match the last-generation model on an eval suite with a tenth of the training compute, for $2B.
- **Private options:** a lab and one neolab agree on a scoped goal, with a buy option and a fallback fee. The author cites SpaceX's option on Cursor ($60B to buy, $10B for the work otherwise).

## Why labs would commit

Training recipes can't be patented, so once a team shows how it did something, the lab no longer needs the team. This is Arrow's information paradox. Naming a price up front lets the team reveal results safely and lets the lab verify before paying.

## Who wins

Labs keep scaling while still betting on breakthroughs, neolabs skip product and go-to-market work, and investors get a committed buyer at a known price. That wouldn't justify a $5B seed round but could justify a $100M one.
