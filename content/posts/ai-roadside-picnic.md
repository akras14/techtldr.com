**Bottom line:** Polat uses the Strugatsky brothers' novel *Roadside Picnic* as a lens on AI-generated science. In the book, aliens visit, leave behind incomprehensible artifacts, and a black market grows around using them without understanding them. AI labs are now doing something similar, most visibly in mathematics, and the open question is whether the pace of discovery will split off from our ability to understand it.

- **The analogy:** A character likens the alien visit to a picnic: travelers leave litter in a meadow, and the animals watching have no idea what it is or whether the visitors will return. Humans in the novel power cars with artifacts they can't explain.
- **The current dumps:** Labs are claiming progress on millennium prize problems and releasing discoveries in batches so large they publish explainers just to navigate them. This arrives faster than the field can absorb it and outside its usual norms. Polat expects such "discovery dumps" to spread to any field where results can be verified in a closed loop.
- **Answers before understanding:** Quoting Sakeeb Rahman, "proof will become cheap and understanding will become the bottleneck." NYU mathematician Tristan Buckmaster said a recent batch of AI papers wiped out entire research programs overnight.
- **A possible anti-accelerationist case:** If AI outruns our ability to grasp its significance, the collateral damage may outweigh the pure scientific value.
- **Difference from the novel:** An alien visit is outside our control, while AI development isn't. Polat is skeptical that coordinated restraint is realistic given the game theory of frontier AI, but says labs can choose to engage scientific communities so understanding keeps pace, and to build models that augment scientists rather than race ahead of them.

Polat says plainly he is not a science historian or philosopher, so this is a framing rather than a worked-out argument.
