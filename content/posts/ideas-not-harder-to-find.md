**Bottom line:** Mastroianni, a psychologist, argues that "ideas are getting harder to find" is an unproven and probably harmful belief. Nobody has shown direct evidence for it, and the belief could discourage the discovery it claims is running out.

**Seven arguments:**
1. **Bias.** Past ideas look obvious in hindsight and future ones look obscure, so we would feel that ideas are running out even if they weren't.
2. **We've been wrong before.** Leading physicists in the 1890s, surgeons in 1873 and early AI researchers all predicted their field was nearly done.
3. **Discoveries beget discoveries.** Knowledge is not a fixed list ordered from easy to hard. Each finding opens new questions.
4. **New discoveries compress old ones.** You don't need to relearn the history of science to work at the frontier.
5. **The world changes.** New phenomena keep needing explanation.
6. **Old discoveries are often wrong.** Failed replications put old fruit back on the tree.
7. **New tools** make old questions answerable.

**Evidence from his own work.** Two papers in a top journal came from very simple ideas. The hard part is selecting from many weak ones: he pitched 59 ideas in two years and only 2 became projects. He also objects to the main economics paper on the topic, which shows output per researcher falling as researcher numbers grow. He finds its measures dubious and the result unsurprising.

**Better model.** Science is not foraging or mining but like elevators. Each paradigm hits a ceiling and gets replaced by something different (balloons, helicopters, rockets) rather than by a taller version of the same thing.
