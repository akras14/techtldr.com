On Zotero's 20th anniversary, co-creator Dan Cohen recounts how a group of historians spent about five years figuring out what the research tool should be before shipping it in 2006. He argues the slow, collaborative start is why it lasted, and that you can't prompt your way to software you don't yet know you want.

## The thesis

Zotero, the free tool for collecting, organizing and citing research, now has more than 20 million users. Cohen says AI couldn't have sped up its conception because the team didn't know exactly what it wanted, so it couldn't have written a coherent prompt. The vision came from years of conversation, which gave the software a strong foundation.

## Two half-solutions (2001–2003)

At George Mason's Center for History and New Media, founded by historian Roy Rosenzweig:

- **Web Scrapbook**, a PHP app by Cohen, captured web resources via a bookmarklet. It was popular in classes but not rigorous; the first version sent passwords unencrypted.
- **Scribe**, a FileMaker app by Elena Razlogova, handled notes and citations locally and became a free EndNote alternative among historians.

By late 2003 the goal was to "webify" Scribe: a research tool that lives in the browser and understands scholarly metadata on the pages you visit.

## Firefox makes it possible

Firefox shipped on 9 November 2004 with XUL, which let developers extend the browser. That night Cohen emailed the team "Firefox + XUL + AWS" (Amazon's book-data API, not the cloud). A grant proposal, "SmartFox: the Scholar's Browser for Digital Collections," followed.

- Teenage developer Simon Kornblith and David Norton built a 0.0.1 prototype by July 2005. Kornblith noticed Firefox would add mozStorage, which became its SQLite support.
- Dan Stillman joined in 2006 and is still Zotero's lead developer. Sean Takats became its long-time director.

## The name

"SmartFox" became "Firefox Scholar" until a lawyer warned against using "Firefox." Rejected ideas included iScholar, Scholr, Citopia and CiteHound. On a colleague's suggestion they consulted an Albanian dictionary and picked *zotero*, "to learn well or master," with every domain available.

## Launch

The beta launched on 5 October 2006 and had 60,000 users within a month. Mellon and Sloan funding later added sync and independence from Firefox. Rosenzweig died in 2007. Zotero 10.0 shipped in August 2026.
