**Bottom line:** PopVax dosed the first participants of its broadly-protective COVID-19 mRNA vaccine candidate PVX-001 in a Phase 1 trial in Melbourne on August 31, 2026. As of October 6, 14 of a planned 36 healthy adults have been dosed, with no serious adverse events reported. After the trial, the company says it will open-source the design and manufacturing information.

## What's different about it

- **Design:** The mRNA makes the body's own cells build a virus-like particle (VLP) that displays an engineered SARS-CoV-2 spike receptor-binding domain. VLP display tends to give stronger and broader antibody responses, but VLP vaccines are normally slow to manufacture (Gardasil 9 takes up to four years). Using mRNA cuts production to about a week.
- **Mouse data:** The company reports more than 50x higher neutralizing antibody titers than the same dose of mRNA encoding the bare immunogen. Its wild-type construct neutralized the Gamma, Lambda and Omicron BA.1 variants where an approved mRNA vaccine comparator did not. It says the current version is competitive with Moderna's mNEXSPIKE in mice.
- **Storage:** A new ionizable lipid (PVXL-150) lets the vaccine sit at 2-8 °C for at least 9 months without freeze-drying, which would make distribution in developing countries much easier.
- **Manufacturing:** Doses were made at the company's RNA Foundry in Hyderabad, filled into vials in Maryland, and shipped to Australia. The facility can make up to 1 million doses a year.

## Funding and openness

Early funding came from the Gates Foundation, with substantial money from Vitalik Buterin's Balvi fund and later investment from Good Ventures. The vaccine is part of NIAID's Project NextGen. After the trial, PopVax says it will publish the design and manufacturing information and won't enforce its IP against anyone making vaccines against betacoronaviruses. Its stated aim is to leave the world a better head start for the next coronavirus. The trial tests safety and early immune response only. Efficacy is unproven.
