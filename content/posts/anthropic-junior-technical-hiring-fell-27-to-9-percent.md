**Bottom line:** Anthropic studied its own workforce as a leading indicator of AI's effect on jobs. It is hiring far fewer junior technical people, output per person is up sharply, and human attention is now the constraint.

**Hiring.** The share of junior employees in technical departments slipped from 21% to 14% since early 2025, and the junior share of technical hires fell from 27% to 9%. Talk of weaker demand for juniors began in January 2026, and by April engineering and research teams had deprioritized lower-band roles. Technical departments fell from 61% to 56% of the company as sales, finance and recruiting hiring outpaced them. Anthropic earns 2-4x the revenue per employee that Meta and Google did at comparable growth stages.

**Agents.** Agent work hours passed human hours in March 2026; by August, employee-directed interactive agents worked six times as many hours as their human handlers. Employees produced more code, closed more sales and processed more contracts after adopting agents. Coding-agent sessions by non-technical staff went from 2 per month (August 2025) to 90 (May 2026), and administrative roles have barely grown.

**Bottleneck.** With so much agent output, reviewing it all would take even non-technical staff about half their week, so people build "meta-work" visibility and monitoring tools. Hiring managers now stress autonomy and judgment over specific technical expertise or implementation skill.

**Response.** Anthropic set up an early-warning system for jobs at risk and prioritizes internal redeployment; staff with a year or more of tenure were 50% more likely to switch departments each month in 2026 than in 2025. The data covers internal records plus 17 employee interviews, so it describes one unusually AI-heavy company rather than the wider market.
