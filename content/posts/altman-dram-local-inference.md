**Bottom line:** Sam Altman says the world's limited DRAM is better used in cloud data centers than in personal computers, where he says it is used only about 5% of the time.

- **The argument:** Many people assume open-source models running locally will ease demand for data center memory. Altman says that is backwards: "from a global allocation perspective, actually, it's quite terrible to have everyone have a bunch of the world's limited supply of DRAM sitting in their own computer." In a constrained supply you want maximum utilization in the cloud.
- **Context:** The article ties this to a year of sharply higher memory prices driven by AI demand, Micron's exit from consumer memory, and a claim that OpenAI may buy up to 40% of global DRAM output. The article does not source that figure, so treat it with caution.
- **Custom chip:** Altman also said OpenAI is designing its own AI processor, called Jalapeno, with AI doing the design work. He argued that since silicon capacity is limited, everyone shares an interest in using it well.
- **Caveat:** The write-up is opinionated and comes from a games site rather than the interview itself, so the quotes are as reported.
