**Bottom line:** This is an advocacy page, so read it as the campaign's case rather than a neutral report. It says that from 2027 Google will require every Android app developer to register centrally before their software can be installed on any device, not only Play Store apps, and that unregistered apps will be silently blocked worldwide.

**What the campaign says registration involves**

- Paying Google a fee and agreeing to its terms.
- Handing over government-issued ID and evidence of the private signing key.
- Listing all current and future application identifiers.

**Who it says is hurt:** users, who could only run Google-approved software on phones they already own; hobbyists, volunteers and internal-app teams; and F-Droid, which has called the change an "existential" threat. It also points to Google's record of complying with government takedown demands.

**The "escape hatch":** Google says power users can still install unverified apps. The page describes the flow as nine steps, including enabling Developer Mode, restarting, a mandatory 24-hour wait, and choosing temporary (7 days) or indefinite permission. It adds that this flow runs through Google Play Services rather than the OS, so Google could tighten it without an OS update.

**Its rebuttal to the security rationale:** Play Protect already scans for malware regardless of developer identity, and malware authors can register while independent developers and dissidents often cannot. The page cites the EFF, Cory Doctorow and Ars Technica in support. Google's side of the argument is not presented on the page.
