The report from the Houston Advanced Research Center models four power-supply scenarios through 2031. Emissions range from a marginal increase to a reversal of progress made since 2002, depending on whether data centers draw on the grid with wind and solar, or on their own natural gas.

- **Cleanest case:** statewide nitrogen oxides still rise 35% by 2031.
- **Gas-heavy cases:** one scenario has NOx up 136%, near 2004 levels. On-site gas engines emit about four times the NOx of grid power for the same facility, and the worst case is nearly +400%. Volatile organic compounds would pass their 2002 level by 32%, and particulates would return to 2006 levels.
- **Why gas:** at least 288 data center projects are planned in Texas. ERCOT received over 500 large-load connection requests in two years, against about two dozen the year before, and the planned grid demand adds up to 439 GW. With grid waits of three to five years, developers build their own generation, often not the cleanest kind.

Gov. Greg Abbott has temporarily halted new data center grid connections pending an audit of power, water, public aid and community impact. The researchers urge treating data centers as industrial facilities held to the same emissions standards, and requiring disclosure of power sources and pollution before projects are approved.
